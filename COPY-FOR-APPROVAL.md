## Practice area 01 — Divorce  ⚖️ attorney review

*Summary:* Strategic guidance through the Louisiana divorce process, from planning before filing to final judgment.

A divorce involving significant assets rarely turns on the divorce itself. It turns on the decisions made around it: when to file, how and when the community property regime will end, what support may be owed while the case is pending, and how the financial record is assembled. Ms. Harris advises clients from the earliest stage — often before anyone has filed — so that those decisions are made deliberately rather than in haste.

In Louisiana, a divorce is generally granted after the spouses have lived separate and apart for a period of time set by law. The required period depends on the circumstances, including whether there are minor children of the marriage and whether the couple entered into a covenant marriage. Certain grounds, such as adultery, can allow a divorce without waiting. Ms. Harris will explain how the rules apply to your situation and what the timing means for your property and support.

Most matters are best resolved through careful negotiation, and she pursues a fair agreement wherever it serves her client. When agreement is not possible, she is prepared to litigate. In either case, you will understand each step before it happens.

**What Ms. Harris reviews** (list on the area's own page)
- Planning and timing before a petition is filed
- Protecting financial records and accounts early
- Interim support and use of the family home while the case is pending
- Negotiated settlements and, when necessary, trial

**Common questions**

**Q. Should I speak with an attorney before I move out or file?**  
Often, yes. Early decisions — about the family home, bank and investment accounts, business operations, and the timing of a filing — can affect property and support later. A consultation before you act gives you the chance to plan.

**Q. How long does a divorce take in Louisiana?**  
It depends on the type of divorce and your circumstances. In most cases the law requires a period of living separate and apart, and resolving property and support issues can take longer than the divorce itself. Ms. Harris will give you a realistic sense of timing at your consultation.

**Q. Does the divorce also divide our property?**  
Not necessarily. The divorce ends the marriage; dividing community property is often handled separately, either by agreement or by the court. Ms. Harris will explain how the two proceedings fit together in your case.

## Practice area 02 — Complex Community Property Partitions  ⚖️ attorney review

*Search listing* — **Title:** Community Property Attorney, Lafayette LA | Helen Popich Harris  
**Description:** Louisiana community property partitions involving businesses, professional practices, real estate, investments and reimbursement claims. Lafayette, LA.

*Summary:* Identifying, classifying, valuing, and dividing community assets and debts — including businesses, real estate, and reimbursement claims.

Louisiana is a community property state. In general, property acquired by either spouse during the marriage is presumed to belong to the community, while property owned before the marriage — or received individually by inheritance or donation — is generally separate. When the community ends, its assets and debts must be identified, classified, valued, and divided. In a complex estate, each of those steps can be contested.

Ms. Harris handles partitions involving closely held businesses and professional practices, commingled accounts, real estate, investment portfolios, and deferred compensation. She evaluates reimbursement claims — for example, where separate funds were used for the community's benefit, or community funds were used to pay a separate debt or improve separate property — along with issues such as the use of the family home while the partition is pending.

Because these cases are as much financial as legal, she works closely with CPAs, forensic accountants, and appraisers to trace funds, value assets, and present the numbers clearly — whether across the negotiating table or before the court.

**What Ms. Harris reviews** (list on the area's own page)
- Classification of community and separate property
- Closely held businesses and professional practices
- Commingled funds and tracing
- Reimbursement claims between the community and separate estates
- Use of the family home pending partition
- Real estate, investment portfolios, and debts

**Common questions**

**Q. What is the difference between community and separate property?**  
Generally, community property is what either spouse acquired during the marriage through effort, skill, or income. Separate property generally includes what a spouse owned before the marriage and what was received individually by inheritance or donation. Classification can become complicated when separate and community funds are mixed, so the facts and the records matter.

**Q. What is a reimbursement claim?**  
A reimbursement claim seeks to balance the accounts when one estate benefited at the expense of the other — for example, when separate funds were used to pay a community debt, or community funds were used to improve one spouse's separate property. These claims can significantly affect the final division.

**Q. Who may live in the family home before the property is divided?**  
Louisiana law allows a court, in appropriate circumstances, to award one spouse the use and occupancy of the family home while the partition is pending. Whether rent or other obligations may be owed depends on the facts and on the court's orders.

## Practice area 04 — Spousal Support  ⚖️ attorney review

*Search listing* — **Title:** Spousal Support Attorney in Lafayette, LA | Helen Popich Harris  
**Description:** Interim and final periodic spousal support in Louisiana — claims, defenses, and modifications — from Lafayette family law attorney Helen Popich Harris.  
**Attorney reviewed:** **not yet**

*Summary:* Interim and final periodic support — pursuing or defending claims, and modifying awards when circumstances change.

Louisiana recognizes two principal forms of spousal support. Interim periodic support is intended to help a spouse maintain the standard of living enjoyed during the marriage while the divorce is pending, based on that spouse's needs and the other spouse's ability to pay. It is temporary and generally ends within a limited time after the divorce is granted, subject to exceptions.

Final periodic support may be awarded after the divorce to a spouse who is in need and who was free from fault before the divorce proceeding was filed. Courts weigh a range of factors — among them income and means, earning capacity, health and age, the duration of the marriage, and the effect of child custody on a spouse's ability to work — and the law limits the amount that may be awarded.

Support and property are connected. The partition of community property can change each spouse's means and needs, so Ms. Harris approaches support as part of the overall financial picture. She represents both spouses who seek support and those asked to pay it, and handles requests to modify or terminate support when circumstances materially change.

**What Ms. Harris reviews** (list on the area's own page)
- Interim periodic support while the divorce is pending
- Final periodic support after divorce
- Documenting need, income, and ability to pay
- The connection between support and the property partition
- Modification and termination of existing awards

**Common questions**

**Q. What is the difference between interim and final periodic support?**  
Interim support is temporary and is intended to bridge the period while the divorce is pending. Final periodic support may be awarded after the divorce based on need and other factors, and it is available only to a spouse who was free from fault in the breakup of the marriage.

**Q. Can spousal support be changed later?**  
An award can generally be modified or terminated when there is a material change in circumstances. Certain events, such as the remarriage of the spouse receiving support, ordinarily end final periodic support.

## Practice area 05 — Retirement Division & QDROs  ⚖️ attorney review

*Search listing* — **Title:** QDRO & Retirement Division, Lafayette LA | Helen Popich Harris  
**Description:** Division of pensions, 401(k)s, IRAs, and other retirement benefits in Louisiana divorces, including Qualified Domestic Relations Orders (QDROs).  
**Attorney reviewed:** **not yet**

*Summary:* Dividing pensions, 401(k)s, IRAs, and other retirement assets — with orders drafted precisely to avoid costly mistakes.

For many couples, retirement benefits are among the most valuable assets in the community. Benefits earned during the marriage are generally community property, even when the account or pension is in only one spouse's name. Dividing them correctly requires both an accurate allocation and an order the plan administrator will accept.

Many employer-sponsored plans can be divided only through a Qualified Domestic Relations Order, or QDRO — a court order directing the plan to pay a share of the benefits to the former spouse. Individual retirement accounts are generally divided by a transfer incident to divorce, and government and military retirement systems follow their own rules and procedures. Precision matters. An order that is vague about valuation dates, investment gains and losses, survivor benefits, or cost-of-living adjustments can cost a client a substantial sum, and a mistake in how funds are transferred can trigger avoidable taxes and penalties. Ms. Harris addresses these details at the outset and works with plan administrators and, when appropriate, actuaries, so that the division is carried out as intended.

**What Ms. Harris reviews** (list on the area's own page)
- Defined-benefit pensions and defined-contribution plans such as 401(k)s
- Individual retirement accounts
- Government and military retirement [[CONFIRM: scope]]
- Qualified Domestic Relations Orders (QDROs)
- Survivor benefits, gains and losses, and cost-of-living adjustments
- Avoiding unnecessary taxes and penalties on transfers

**Common questions**

**Q. What is a QDRO?**  
A Qualified Domestic Relations Order is a court order that allows a retirement plan governed by federal law to pay part of a participant's benefits to a former spouse. The plan administrator must determine that the order meets the plan's requirements before it is treated as qualified.

**Q. How is a pension earned partly during the marriage divided?**  
Louisiana courts commonly apply a fractional formula that compares the time the benefit was earned during the community with the total period of service. The details — including how the order defines the benefit being divided — can be significant.

**Q. When should the QDRO be prepared?**  
Ideally, its terms are addressed while the property settlement is negotiated, and the order is prepared and submitted promptly afterward. Delay can create problems — for example, if the participant retires, dies, or takes a distribution before the order is in place.

## Practice area 06 — Child Support  ⚖️ attorney review

*Search listing* — **Title:** Child Support Attorney in Lafayette, LA | Helen Popich Harris  
**Description:** Child support under the Louisiana guidelines, including business owners, the self-employed and complex compensation. Lafayette attorney Helen Popich Harris.  
**Attorney reviewed:** **not yet**

*Summary:* Support calculated accurately under Louisiana's guidelines — including cases involving self-employment and complex compensation.

Louisiana calculates child support using statutory guidelines based on the combined adjusted gross income of both parents and the number of children. The basic obligation is divided between the parents in proportion to their incomes, and certain expenses — such as health insurance, child care, extraordinary medical expenses, and, in some cases, private school tuition or special needs — may be added to it. When the parents' combined income exceeds the highest level of the guidelines schedule, the court has discretion in setting support, guided by the best interest of the children and each parent's circumstances.

In many of Ms. Harris's cases, the central question is what counts as income. Business owners and the self-employed may have income that is not obvious from a tax return; executives may receive bonuses, deferred compensation, or equity awards; and a parent who is voluntarily unemployed or underemployed may have income attributed to them. Getting the income right is the foundation of a fair award.

Ms. Harris handles initial support determinations as well as requests to modify an existing award when there has been a material change in circumstances. Please note that her practice does not include contested child custody matters.

**What Ms. Harris reviews** (list on the area's own page)
- Calculations under the Louisiana child support guidelines
- Income of business owners and the self-employed
- Bonuses, deferred compensation, and equity awards
- Health insurance, child care, and extraordinary expenses
- Combined incomes above the guidelines schedule
- Modification of existing awards

**Common questions**

**Q. How is child support calculated in Louisiana?**  
Support is calculated under statutory guidelines using both parents' combined adjusted gross income and the number of children. The resulting obligation is shared in proportion to each parent's income, and certain expenses may be added to it.

**Q. What if a parent's income is hard to determine?**  
Self-employment, business ownership, and complex compensation can make income difficult to measure. The guidelines address business expenses and in-kind benefits, and financial records — and sometimes expert analysis — are often needed to establish an accurate figure.

**Q. Can child support be changed?**  
Yes. Either parent may ask the court to modify support when there has been a material change in circumstances since the last order.
