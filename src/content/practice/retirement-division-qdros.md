For many couples, retirement benefits are among the most valuable assets in the community. Benefits earned during the marriage are generally community property, even when the account or pension is in only one spouse's name. Dividing them correctly requires both an accurate allocation and an order the plan administrator will accept.

Many employer-sponsored plans can be divided only through a Qualified Domestic Relations Order, or QDRO — a court order directing the plan to pay a share of the benefits to the former spouse. Individual retirement accounts are generally divided by a transfer incident to divorce, and government and military retirement systems follow their own rules and procedures. [[CONFIRM: scope of government and military retirement work]]

Precision matters. An order that is vague about valuation dates, investment gains and losses, survivor benefits, or cost-of-living adjustments can cost a client a substantial sum, and a mistake in how funds are transferred can trigger avoidable taxes and penalties. Ms. Harris addresses these details at the outset and works with plan administrators and, when appropriate, actuaries, so that the division is carried out as intended.
